Heightened CNC Press Brake in Red: Built for Tall-Flange & Deep-Box Parts

Today's update: a heightened CNC press brake in production — grey-black body with a red heightened structure, featuring an enlarged daylight and longer ram stroke for tall-flange workpieces.

1. Why Does a Press Brake Need to Be Heightened?

A standard CNC press brake typically offers an open height (daylight — the working space between the upper and lower tools) of around 300–450 mm, which is enough for most common bending jobs. Problems appear when the workpiece has tall flanges or deep box sections — for example tall cabinet panels, deep U-channels, door frames or large covers. During positioning or turning, the tall bent flange collides with the ram or the top beam, so the part simply cannot be placed or bent.

A heightened press brake solves this with a taller frame, longer ram stroke and enlarged daylight, reserving generous vertical clearance so tall-flange parts can be bent in one setup.

2. How to Tell Whether Your Workpiece Needs a Heightened Machine

The rule of thumb:

Required daylight ≥ Height of the tallest flange + Lower die height + Upper punch height + Safety margin (50–100 mm recommended)

Choose a heightened model if any of the following applies:

  1. Tall bent flanges — the finished upright flange is taller than the daylight of a standard machine;
  2. The part must be lifted or turned over the tooling — especially box-type and deep-cavity parts, which have to be raised above the punch to bend the next side;
  3. The product itself is deep or tall — door frames, elevator decoration panels, cabinet side panels, hoppers, large covers;
  4. Multi-bend parts — every additional bend and turnover increases interference risk; extra height is the safer choice.

💡 Not sure whether your part will collide? Send us the drawing or dimensions — our engineers will calculate the required daylight and recommend the right machine, free of charge.

3. Benefits of a Heightened Press Brake

  1. Tall and deep parts finished in one setup — no outsourcing, no second clamping;
  2. Easier positioning and turning — generous working space lowers the risk of collisions and scratches, protecting surface quality;
  3. More orders accepted — take the deep-box and tall-cabinet jobs that standard machines cannot handle and grow your business;
  4. One machine, dual duty — a heightened model bends standard parts just as well, large or small;
  5. Higher efficiency and yield — less rework and trial bending caused by interference.
